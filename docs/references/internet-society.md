## What the Internet Society is

The Internet Society (ISOC, founded 1992 by Internet pioneers, organizational
home of the IETF) is a global, community-driven nonprofit whose founding
vision is that *the Internet is for everyone*. It works through chapters,
members and partners on connectivity for the ~2.2 billion still unconnected,
internet governance, security, privacy, and policy — defending an **open,
globally connected, secure and trustworthy Internet**.

- About: https://www.internetsociety.org/about-internet-society/
- The Internet Way of Networking / Internet Impact Assessment Toolkit:
  https://www.internetsociety.org/issues/internet-way-of-networking/ —
  defines the **critical properties** of the Internet (an accessible
  infrastructure with a common protocol; open architecture of interoperable
  building blocks; decentralized management and a single distributed
  routing system; common global identifiers; a technology-neutral,
  general-purpose network) and a method for assessing whether a policy or
  technology strengthens or erodes them.
- APDSI (author of the UN Charter-reform letter preserved in
  [apdsi-un-charter-reform.md](apdsi-un-charter-reform.md)) is the
  Portuguese association for the promotion and development of the
  information society — the same civil-society family: both argue that the
  infrastructure of open information is a public good requiring active
  defense.

## Why this matters to a peace-intelligence platform

1. **The platform's operating assumption is an open Internet.** Global
   ingestion from institutional sources, public verification trails, and a
   feed readable from every country presume a globally connected network.
   Internet fragmentation ("splinternet"), national shutdowns and blocking
   directly degrade early-warning coverage — a shutdown in a crisis region
   is itself a conflict signal the platform should learn to observe.
2. **Open information access is prevention.** The Global Digital Compact's
   information-integrity commitments, ISOC's open-Internet defense, and this
   platform's cited public feed are the same argument at three altitudes:
   communities with access to independent, fact-based information are harder
   to mobilize into violence.
3. **Design alignment.** The platform follows the properties ISOC defends:
   open standards (indicator 8 of the DPG Standard —
   [dpg-alliance.md](dpg-alliance.md)), no proprietary lock-in, public APIs,
   decentralized verifiability (anyone can audit a trail), AGPL-3.0 so the
   commons keeps its improvements.

## Practical hooks (future work)

- Use the Internet Impact Assessment Toolkit as a checklist whenever the
  platform considers features that touch identity, blocking or content
  control (e.g. reviewer authentication, source blocklists).
- Internet shutdown / connectivity-disruption data (e.g. ISOC Pulse,
  https://pulse.internetsociety.org) is a candidate ingestion source: a
  structured, open signal of information blackouts in conflict zones —
  directly relevant to the `humanitarian_access` vital and to source-coverage
  honesty (a silent country is not a peaceful country).
