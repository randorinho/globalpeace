# United Nations — Peace & Digital Governance Framework

> **Reference document** (information_type: `interpretation` — summaries of
> official UN documents, with links to the originals; not platform policy).
> Added 2026-11-01. Companion to
> [apdsi-un-charter-reform.md](apdsi-un-charter-reform.md), the civil-society
> letter arguing the UN Charter must learn to see autonomous systems.

The three UN instruments below are the platform's primary institutional
anchors: they are what globalpeace.ai supports and plugs into — the platform
supports existing UN structures for prevention, mediation, peacebuilding and
peacekeeping; it does not replace them (README).

## 1. Pact for the Future (A/RES/79/1, 22 September 2024)

The outcome document of the Summit of the Future, adopted by consensus by the
General Assembly: 56 actions across sustainable development, international
peace and security, science and technology, youth and future generations, and
global governance. Its annexes are the **Global Digital Compact** (Annex I)
and the **Declaration on Future Generations** (Annex II).

- Official text and overview: https://www.un.org/en/summit-of-the-future
- Why it matters here: it is the current, consensus-adopted frame tying
  *peace and security* to *technology governance* — the same coupling this
  platform operationalizes (treated conflict events → human-accountable
  preventive options).

## 2. A New Agenda for Peace (Policy Brief 9, 20 July 2023)

The Secretary-General's vision for multilateral peace and security ahead of
the Summit of the Future. Grounded in the UN Charter; built on trust,
solidarity and universality.

- Source: https://dppa.un.org/en/a-new-agenda-for-peace
- Key recommendations relevant to this platform:
  - **Prevention first** — shift from reaction to prevention, including
    national prevention strategies and regional frameworks. This is the
    platform's founding premise (docs/ROADMAP.md: detect → confirm →
    explain → suggest proportionate responses).
  - **Weaponization of new technologies** named as a central risk, with the
    Secretary-General's call to conclude a legally binding instrument on
    lethal autonomous weapons — the same ground the APDSI letter radicalizes
    into Charter reform (Arts. 86-A–86-H).
  - **Violence outside war zones and information threats** treated as peace
    and security concerns — mirrored in the platform's event taxonomy
    (protest, attack, negotiation, displacement) and its
    disinformation-correction playbook (docs/ARCHITECTURE.md).

## 3. Global Digital Compact (Annex I of A/RES/79/1)

The first comprehensive global framework for digital cooperation and AI
governance, negotiated by all 193 Member States.

- Source: https://www.un.org/global-digital-compact/en
- Commitments this platform directly serves:
  - **Information integrity**: countering misinformation through access to
    "independent, fact-based and timely information" — the public feed is
    exactly that: verified events, cited sources, public verification trails.
  - **International law and human rights online** as the baseline for
    digital spaces — the platform's Human Rights Agent publishes positions
    and audits the platform itself (docs/HUMAN_RIGHTS_AGENT.md).
  - **AI governance with human accountability** — interoperable oversight,
    scientific panels; locally: machine opinions never verify, every audit
    carries `human_review_required`, dispatch is always human
    (docs/AUDIT_CRITERIA.md, docs/GOVERNANCE.md).
- Follow-up: implementation mapping from 2025; high-level review at the
  82nd General Assembly session — a window for registered Digital Public
  Goods (see [dpg-alliance.md](dpg-alliance.md)) to be counted as
  implementation evidence.

## How the three documents relate to the APDSI letter

The UN instruments and the APDSI letter agree on the diagnosis — ungoverned
autonomous power is a peace problem — and differ on remedy: the UN texts work
through cooperation frameworks inside the existing Charter; the APDSI letter
argues the Charter itself must be amended (Art. 109 revision conference).
The platform takes no institutional position; it implements the principles
both share: primacy of human responsibility, explanation and contestation
rights, no automatic action, transparency as default.
